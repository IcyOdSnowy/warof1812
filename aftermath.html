Module 08

Aftermath & The Birth of Identity

The war acted as a "crucible," forging a distinct sense of being Canadian. Living through the American invasion made colonists realize they were not Americans. It sparked a shared history and a common pride in defending their homes, laying the groundwork for Confederation 50 years later.

 Module 09

Loyalty & French-English Unity

Why did loyalty to Britain increase? Fear of American republicanism pushed Canadians closer to the Crown. Surprisingly, French-Canadians and English-Canadians fought side-by-side (notably at Chateauguay), proving they could unite against a common threat to protect their unique way of life.

 Module 10

Why We Remember Today

We remember because without this war, Canada likely wouldn't exist as a separate country. It defined our borders and ensured that North America would remain a place of two distinct nations.
