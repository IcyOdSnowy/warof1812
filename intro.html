What was the War of 1812?

The War of 1812 was a massive conflict fought between the United States and Great Britain (including its colonies in British North America, which we now call Canada).

Often called the "forgotten war," it lasted for two and a half years. It wasn't just a fight over land; it was a fight for respect, trade rights, and the future of the North American continent. For many living in Canada, it was the first time people of different backgrounds—English, French, and Indigenous—united to defend their homes.

Who & Why?

The Combatants: The U.S. declared war on Britain. The British were supported by their North American colonies and a powerful confederacy of First Nations led by Tecumseh.

The Motive: The U.S. was angry about British interference with their trade and the "kidnapping" of their sailors (impressment).

When & Where?

Timeline: The war officially began in June 1812 and ended with a peace treaty in February 1815.

Geography: Most of the fighting happened along the border between the U.S. and Canada, including the Great Lakes, the St. Lawrence River, and even as far south as New Orleans.
