Module 02

The Origins of Conflict.

A global struggle between empires fueled by trade, territory, and a fight for sovereignty.

Long-Term Causes

The roots of the war were planted long before 1812, largely driven by global power struggles and American growth.

  • Napoleonic Wars: Britain and France's global struggle led to naval blockades that crippled neutral trade.
  • Trade Restrictions: British "Orders in Council" required neutral ships to pay fees in Britain.
  • U.S. Expansionism: Americans desired land in Canada and Indigenous territories.

Immediate Causes

Tensions broke due to violations of sovereignty and political pressure at home.

  • Impressment: The Royal Navy seized American sailors, claiming they were British deserters.
  • First Nations Conflict: British support for Indigenous resistance in the Northwest Territory infuriated the US.
  • Jingoism: "War Hawks" in Congress pushed for war to restore national honor.

Impressment

Forcing men into military service; a primary grievance against the British Navy.

Jingoism

Extreme, aggressive patriotism, often pushing for a warlike foreign policy.

Sovereignty

The independent authority of a state to govern its own territory and people.
